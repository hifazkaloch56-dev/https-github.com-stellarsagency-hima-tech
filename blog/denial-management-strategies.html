5 Denial Management Strategies That Actually Work

Published August 2026

Claim denials can create a major challenge for healthcare practices. Even when a practice is providing excellent patient care, preventable billing problems can delay reimbursement and create unnecessary administrative work.

A strong denial prevention and follow-up process helps practices identify recurring problems, recover unpaid claims, and improve the overall performance of the revenue cycle.

Key takeaway: The goal should not only be to appeal denied claims. Practices should identify why denials are happening and prevent the same problems from occurring again.

1. Identify the Root Cause of Every Denial

One of the most important steps is understanding why claims are being denied. Simply resubmitting a claim without analyzing the reason can result in repeated denials and wasted staff time.

Practices should categorize denials by common causes such as eligibility issues, authorization problems, coding errors, missing information, duplicate claims, timely filing issues, and payer-specific requirements.

Once the most common denial categories are identified, the billing team can focus on fixing the underlying workflow rather than treating every denial as an isolated event.

2. Verify Patient Eligibility Before Submission

Eligibility-related problems are among the issues that can create avoidable claim delays. Insurance coverage can change, terminate, or require specific benefits verification.

Verifying eligibility before services are billed can help practices identify coverage issues earlier and reduce unnecessary claim submissions.

  • Confirm active insurance coverage.
  • Verify the patient's member information.
  • Check applicable benefits and coverage limitations.
  • Confirm payer requirements before claim submission.

3. Improve Coding and Documentation Accuracy

Incorrect coding or incomplete documentation can contribute to payment problems. Billing and coding teams should make sure that submitted claims accurately reflect the services documented in the patient's record.

Regular review of coding patterns can help practices identify recurring errors and improve communication between providers, coders, and billing staff.

4. Follow Up on Denied Claims Quickly

A denial should not simply remain in an accounts receivable queue. Timely follow-up helps practices determine whether a claim can be corrected, resubmitted, appealed, or otherwise resolved.

An organized follow-up process should track the denial reason, payer response, required action, responsible team member, and next follow-up date.

Best practice: Maintain a clear denial work queue so every actionable denial has an owner and a documented next step.

5. Track Denial Trends and Measure Performance

Practices cannot effectively improve their denial process without measuring what is happening. Reviewing denial trends over time can reveal recurring payer, coding, eligibility, or workflow problems.

Useful metrics can include denial volume, denial categories, appeal success, days in accounts receivable, and the amount of revenue associated with denied claims.

Reviewing these metrics regularly gives management a clearer picture of where revenue cycle improvements are needed.

Conclusion

Effective denial management requires more than submitting appeals. Healthcare practices need a process that identifies the root cause of denials, prevents avoidable errors, follows up on unpaid claims, and continuously measures performance.

By combining better front-end verification, accurate coding, organized follow-up, and regular denial analysis, practices can build a more efficient revenue cycle and reduce unnecessary payment delays.
